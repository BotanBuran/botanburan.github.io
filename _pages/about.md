I'm a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Political Science at Georgia State University, specializing in International Relations and minoring in Political Methodology.

My research focuses on alignments and regional security. In my dissertation, *The Myth of Regional Balance of Power*, I investigate the question of why regional states rarely form alignment blocs against potential regional hegemons and examine how states respond to these threats. In this project, I question mainstream assumptions about balancing in regional politics. My broader agenda covers alignments, Middle Eastern politics, alliance reliability, and interstate conflicts. I utilize both quantitative and qualitative methods.

At Georgia State, I teach Introduction to American Government and manage the Political Science Department's website and social media accounts. I also served as President of the Graduate Student Association for a year and worked on projects to improve graduate students' lives.

I hold an M.A. in International Relations from TOBB University of Economics and Technology and a B.A. in International Relations from Sakarya University, and I studied at the Università degli Studi di Firenze as an exchange student.
