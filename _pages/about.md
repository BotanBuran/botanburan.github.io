I'm a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Political Science at Georgia State University, where I specialize in International Relations with a minor in Political Methodology.

My research focuses on alignments and regional security. In my dissertation, *The Myth of Regional Balance of Power*, I examine how states respond to regional threats and question common assumptions about balancing in regional politics. My broader agenda covers alliance reliability, interstate conflict, and the effects of military coups on democracy, with a particular focus on the Middle East and Turkey. I use a mixed-methods approach that combines applied statistics with process tracing and case studies.

At Georgia State, I teach Introduction to American Government, serve as President of the Graduate Student Association, and manage the Political Science Department's website and social media accounts.

I hold an M.A. in International Relations from TOBB University of Economics and Technology and a B.A. in International Relations from Sakarya University, and I studied at the Università degli Studi di Firenze as an exchange student.
